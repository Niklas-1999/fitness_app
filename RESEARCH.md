# Research basis

Every number the app uses for training or nutrition comes from this file, and each one has a source. If a value is a coaching convention or an engineering choice rather than evidence, it is marked **[convention]** or **[engineering]**. Unverified claims do not go into the app.

Status legend: ✅ checked against the primary document · ☑️ checked against the abstract or an official summary.

## 1. Resistance training: ACSM Position Stand (2009) ✅

*American College of Sports Medicine. Progression Models in Resistance Training for Healthy Adults. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2009;41(3):687–708.* (PubMed 19204579. Table 2 was read from the full text.)

| Topic | Recommendation (evidence grade) |
|---|---|
| Novice frequency | Train the entire body 2–3 days/week (A) |
| Intermediate frequency | 3–4 days/week (B); 4 days/week for an upper/lower split (B, hypertrophy) |
| Advanced frequency | 4–6 days/week (C) |
| Strength loading, novice and intermediate | ~60–70% 1RM for 8–12 reps (A) |
| Hypertrophy loading, novice and intermediate | 70–85% 1RM, 8–12 reps, 1–3 sets per exercise (A) |
| Muscular endurance, novice and intermediate | Light loads, 10–15 reps, moderate to high volume (A); advanced: 10–25+ reps (C) |
| Sets, novice | 1–3 sets per exercise (A) |
| **Load progression** | **Increase load by 2–10% once the trainee can do 1–2 reps over the target on two consecutive sessions** (B). Use the lower % for small-muscle exercises and the higher % for large ones. |
| Exercise order | Large before small muscle groups, multi-joint before single-joint (C) |
| Rest | 2–3 min for heavy core lifts, 1–2 min for assistance work (B/C); 1–2 min for novice hypertrophy work (C) |
| Untrained individuals | Loads of 45–50% 1RM or less already increase strength (from the text) |

## 2. Training volume, load and frequency ☑️

- **Volume:** Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (J Sports Sci; 15 studies, 34 treatment groups) found a graded dose-response: more weekly sets produce more hypertrophy, roughly up to ~10+ sets per muscle per week. → **App:** for hypertrophy goals, build weekly sets per muscle group toward ~10 over the first months.
- **Load:** Schoenfeld et al. 2017 (JSCR; 21 studies, all sets to failure) found similar hypertrophy with low (≤60% 1RM) and high loads, while 1RM strength gains were greater with heavy loads. → **App:** hypertrophy and endurance goals may use wide rep ranges, including bodyweight work. Strength goals use heavier loads where equipment allows.
- **Frequency:** Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019 (J Sports Sci) found that when weekly volume is equal, training frequency does not meaningfully change hypertrophy. → **App:** the weekdays the user picks set the split. Weekly volume per muscle is what we track.

## 3. Bodyweight progression ☑️

- Calatayud et al. 2015 (JSCR 29(1):246–253): push-ups and bench press at comparable muscle activity produced similar strength gains over 5 weeks.
- Kotarsky et al. 2018 (JSCR, PubMed 29466268): **progressive calisthenic push-up variations** improved 1RM bench press about as much as bench press training did in moderately trained men (4 weeks, 3×/week).
- → **App:** with no equipment, progress through harder **variations** (a progression ladder). Once a variation reaches the top of its rep range, move to the next variation.

## 4. Autoregulation (RPE / reps in reserve) ☑️

- Helms, Cronin, Storey & Zourdos 2016 (Strength Cond J 38(4):42–49): an RPE scale based on reps in reserve (RIR) is a practical way to regulate intensity set by set. RPE 9 means 1 rep left, RPE 8 means 2 left.
- Later RIR studies: predictions are less accurate further from failure. People typically underpredict RIR by about 1 rep.
- → **App:** after each quest the user taps **EASY / SOLID / HARD / FAILED**. These are simplified RIR bands (≥4 / 2–3 / 0–1 / target not reached). They drive the next session's target.

## 5. Deload ☑️

- Bell et al. 2023 (Sports Med Open, Delphi consensus): deloading is "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness." The authors call it ubiquitous but under-researched.
- → **App:** a lighter week (about half the sets, the same exercises) every ~6th week, or earlier if several HARD/FAILED ratings pile up. **[convention]** The spacing and size are coaching practice, not strict evidence.

## 6. Physical activity guidelines: WHO 2020 ☑️

*Bull et al. Br J Sports Med 2020;54:1451–1462.*
- Adults: 150–300 min/week of moderate or 75–150 min/week of vigorous aerobic activity, plus muscle strengthening of all major muscle groups on ≥2 days/week. Some activity is better than none.
- Adults 65+: the same, plus **varied multicomponent activity emphasising functional balance and strength on ≥3 days/week**.
- → **App:** a weekly "active minutes" side quest toward 150 min. Users 65+ automatically get balance quests 3×/week.

## 7. Cardio progression ✅ (NHS plan read directly)

- **NHS Couch to 5K**: 9 weeks, 3 runs/week with a rest day between. Every run has a 5 min warm-up walk and a 5 min cool-down walk.
  - Wk1: (run 1:00 / walk 1:30) ×7, then run 1:00 · Wk2: (run 1:30 / walk 2:00) ×5, then run 1:30
  - Wk3: run 1:30, walk 1:30, run 3:00, walk 3:00, run 1:30, walk 1:30, run 3:00
  - Wk4: run 3, walk 1:30, run 5, walk 2:30, run 3, walk 1:30, run 5
  - Wk5: R1 5/3/5/3/5 · R2 8/5/8 · R3 20 continuous · Wk6: R1 5/3/8/3/5 · R2 10/3/10 · R3 25 continuous
  - Wk7: 25 continuous · Wk8: 28 · Wk9: 30
- Buist et al. 2008 (Am J Sports Med 36:33–39; GRONORUN RCT): a 13-week program following the "10% rule" gave **no fewer injuries** than a standard 8-week program (20.8% vs 20.3%). → **App:** we do not claim the 10% rule prevents injury. We follow the structured run/walk progression and repeat a week after a FAILED rating.

## 8. Health screening ☑️

- PAR-Q+ (PAR-Q+ Collaboration, 2024 version): 7 general yes/no health questions covering heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or loss of consciousness, other chronic conditions, medication, bone/joint problems, and doctor-supervised activity only. Any YES → consult a doctor or qualified professional.
- → **App:** onboarding asks **paraphrased** versions (the PAR-Q+ text itself is copyrighted). Any YES shows a clear "get medical clearance" notice and starts the plan in conservative mode (lower volume, no HARD targets). The app never gives medical advice.

## 9. Energy needs ✅/☑️

- **BMR, Mifflin–St Jeor 1990** (Am J Clin Nutr 51:241–247; 498 adults aged 19–78):
  `men: 10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + 5` · `women: 10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age − 161`
- Frankenfield et al. 2005 (J Am Diet Assoc 105:775–789, systematic review): Mifflin–St Jeor predicted RMR within 10% of measured for more non-obese and obese people than any other equation, but errors can still be large for individuals. → **App:** the formula is only the *starting* estimate (see adaptive TDEE).
- **Physical activity level (EFSA 2017 DRV summary ✅):** PAL 1.4 / 1.6 / 1.8 / 2.0 = low active (sedentary) / moderately active / active / very active. `TDEE = BMR × PAL`.
- **Weight-change dynamics:** Thomas et al. 2013 and Hall & Chow 2013 (Int J Obes): the static "3500 kcal (7700 kcal/kg) rule" overestimates long-term weight loss because energy expenditure adapts. → **App:** projections are **re-fitted every week** from the logged body-weight trend and calorie intake (adaptive TDEE). We never draw a straight line to the goal.

## 10. Weight-change rates ☑️

- **Fat loss:** Helms, Aragon & Fitschen 2014 (JISSN 11:20): about **0.5–1% of body weight per week** to maximise muscle retention.
- **Muscle gain:** Iraki, Fitschen, Espinar & Helms 2019 (Sports 7(7):154): a surplus of about **10–20% above maintenance**, aiming for **0.25–0.5% of body weight per week** (novice/intermediate). Advanced lifters should be more conservative. The authors note the evidence is thin.
- → **App intensity setting:** fat loss gentle 0.5% / standard 0.75% / hard 1.0% BW per week. Gain gentle 0.25% / standard 0.35% / hard 0.5%.
- **Calorie floor: never set the target below BMR. [engineering safety guard]**

## 11. Macronutrients and water ✅/☑️

- **EFSA DRVs, adults ≥18 (2017 summary ✅):** fat 20–35% of energy · carbohydrate 45–60% of energy · fibre 25 g/day · saturated fat "as low as possible" · protein PRI 0.83 g/kg/day (general population) · water AI **2.0 L/day women, 2.5 L/day men** (total water, including water from food).
- **Protein for people who train:**
  - Morton et al. 2018 (BJSM 52:376–384; 49 studies, 1,863 people): extra protein beyond **~1.6 g/kg/day** gave no further gains in fat-free mass. The confidence interval reaches ~2.2.
  - ISSN Position Stand, Jäger et al. 2017 (JISSN 14:20): **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day** for most exercising people; 0.25 g/kg or 20–40 g per meal, spread every 3–4 h; **2.3–3.1 g/kg** may help keep lean mass in trained people on a calorie deficit.
- → **App:** protein 1.6 g/kg (maintain/gain) and 2.0 g/kg (deficit). For users with BMI > 30 who are losing weight, use the **target** weight as the reference **[engineering choice, avoids extreme gram targets; to be verified]**. Fat 30% of energy (clamped to 20–35%). Carbs fill the rest. Fibre 25 g, water per EFSA.

## 12. EU nutrition labels ☑️

- Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Art. 30 + Annex XV: the mandatory declaration lists, **in this order**: energy (kJ/kcal), fat, of which saturates, carbohydrate, of which sugars, protein, salt, **per 100 g / 100 ml**. Optional extras: mono-/polyunsaturates, polyols, starch, fibre.
- → **App:** the label scanner looks for exactly these 7 fields (plus optional fibre) per 100 g, using German and English keywords (Brennwert/Energie, Fett, davon gesättigte Fettsäuren, Kohlenhydrate, davon Zucker, Eiweiß, Salz, Ballaststoffe). Logging asks for the grams eaten.
- On-device OCR: **Tesseract4Android** (Tesseract 5, runs fully offline; `deu` + `eng` traineddata bundled in the app). The licence must be checked before adding it. Google ML Kit is excluded because it is a Google SDK.

## 13. EU reference intakes (Reg. 1169/2011 Annex XIII, Part B) ☑️

Adult reference intakes used for the "max" info lines: energy 8400 kJ / 2000 kcal, fat 70 g, **saturates 20 g**, carbohydrate 260 g, **sugars 90 g**, protein 50 g, **salt 6 g**.

## 14. Adaptive TDEE [engineering]

Following the critique of the static 7700 kcal/kg rule (§9), targets are not projected from the formula forever. Over a rolling 28-day window, energy balance is measured directly: observed TDEE = mean logged intake − weight slope × 7700 kcal/kg. Over short windows this approximation is reasonable. Errors: incomplete food logging biases TDEE low. The weight slope is a least-squares line, which filters water swings and has no lag. The result is blended with the formula by data coverage and clamped (60–160 %).

## Not used (insufficient evidence for a number)

- **Predicted kg of muscle gained.** The rates vary too much between individuals and studies. The app shows projected *performance* (reps, loads, run times) and *body-weight* trends instead.
- **"10% rule" injury claims** (see Buist 2008).
